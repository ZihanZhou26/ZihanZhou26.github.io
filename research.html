Research

Research Interests

I work at the interdisciplinary interface of high energy physics and astrophysics, with a particular focus on applying ideas from effective field theory and scattering amplitudes to understand black holes, relativistic binary dynamics, tidal disruption events, and fluid mechanics. More recently, my interests have expanded to machine-learning methods for gravitational-wave data analysis and nonequilibrium quantum dynamical systems.

Core Themes

Effective Field Theories (EFT)

Applying EFT ideas to binary black-hole dynamics, tidal disruption events, surface-fluid dynamics, and emergent phases in condensed-matter systems.

Scattering Amplitudes in Gravity

Developing the Gravitational Raman scattering program to systematically characterize compact-object tidal effects in general relativity, and using renormalization-group methods for waveform resummation in strong-field regime.

Gravitational-Wave Data Analysis

Constraining finite-size effects with LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA data, forecasting detectability for future detectors, and developing machine-learning strategies for template-bank construction.

Quantum Dynamics

Exploring nonequilibrium quantum dynamics through Hilbert-space fragmentation and the interplay between generalized symmetries and random matrix universality.
